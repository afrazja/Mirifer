# Speaking first: steps that make the learner talk

Owner's request (2026-10-28): for learners who want to improve their speaking, the steps should make
them talk more, and the steps that do little for speaking should go. Status: **v2, after the
learning-UX and english-coach reviews; waiting for the owner's two answers.**

## Who it is for

The Everyday goal and "Not sure yet: I just want to speak better". Recommended for every new chapter
of every goal; Travel's four existing days stay as they are.

## How much each step makes the learner talk today

Realistic figures (typical answers, not time limits); the real data is too thin to judge (17
recordings in 90 days, and role-play replies aren't measured).

| Step type | Learner's own voice per step | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Listen and connect | 0 s (taps answers) | none |
| Persian-speaker traps | ~15–20 s | says 5 fixed sentences |
| Natural phrases | ~20–30 s | repeating |
| Say it in English (3 items) | ~20–30 s | guided, own words |
| Voice messages (1 message) | ~20–35 s | own words |
| Role-play with a twist | ~40–60 s, and it can be typed | conversation |
| Listen and retell | ~45–60 s | own words |
| Say it again, better ("About you") | ~1–1.3 min (two tries) | own words + fix |
| Quick-fire questions (lab) | ~40–60 s at A2, more at B1 | conversation |

A chapter today: about **1.5–2 minutes** of the learner's own speech, much of it repeating. The new
shape: about **4 minutes**, most of it in their own words, with nothing tap-only.

## What the reviews changed

The kind of talk matters more than the minutes. Most useful first: saying the same thing again after
feedback (try → fix → try); real exchanges (reacting, asking, checking); guided output that recalls
the chapter's phrases from a meaning; repeating (short: it's the pronunciation and chunk practice);
unsupported free talk last (at A2 it mostly brings silence or avoidance). So:

- **Don't delete steps; turn their taps into talk.** The listening check, the easy first win, the
  Persian-speaker traps and the taarof teaching stay, all spoken.
- **Talk time is a measure, not the goal.** A 2-second "Biscuit!" can be the right answer.
- **A2 order:** short answers before the long turn (Quick-fire before About you).

## The rules

1. **Every step is spoken:** no tap-only questions; listening leads to speaking.
2. **Fixes come from the learner's own mistakes, topped up with the chapter's traps:** when the AI
   finds fewer than two fixes from the step's priority list, one chapter trap is added ("Persian
   speakers often say 'Where you are from?'. Say it the natural way.").
3. **A feedback budget, in code:** A2 at most one fix per short-answer step and two per long one
   (about five in a chapter); B1 at most two per step. Nothing during Quick-fire or the role-play;
   fixes at the end of the step, each with "Say it again". Never fix a natural short answer, a choice
   the learner made, a likely speech-to-text slip, or punctuation.
4. **Voice first, with a quiet way out:** the microphone is the main button everywhere. "Can't talk
   now?" on every speaking step: type the answer (counts 0 talk time) or come back to the step later,
   without losing the day.
5. **Calm timings at A2:** tap to answer, no "quick start" score, about 4 s of quiet before an answer
   ends, about 12 s before a question counts as missed; "Let me think…" counts as a start.
6. **Measure honestly:** talk time = voiced seconds (from the mic level, not recording length), in
   English, 3+ words; split into "repeated" and "own words"; typed replies count 0. Admin: per step
   type, speech seconds, words, time to start, skip rate. Learner: one line at most ("You spoke for
   about 4 minutes today."), never a comparison.

## The new chapter shape (Everyday Chapter 1 as the example)

| | Intermediate (A2) | Professional (B1+) | Talk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Listen, answer, tell**: after each of the two parts, one short spoken answer ("What's the dog's name?", "When does the trash go out?"), then say what happened in 2–3 sentences with hint chips. One more listen allowed; the text shows after they speak | **Listen and retell** as now, with a quiet "Need a hint?" (the box · the trash · the barbecue) | ~45–60 s |
| 2 | **Natural phrases + use them**: repeat the five phrases, then two short exchanges with Jake that need them (Jake's line, the intention in Persian, the learner's reply; checked by the phrase's pattern) | The same, the intention in English | ~45–60 s |
| 3 | **Quick-fire with Jake** (six questions from the chapter, calm timings, hint chips; Q2 "Hey, nice plant! Where did you get it?" checks "Thanks!" first) | Six B1 questions (one or two AI follow-ups later) | ~1 min |
| 4 | **About you** (45 s; the second try "Use the fix, and add one more thing"; the Quick-fire answers' key words as idea chips) | **About you** (60 s) | ~1–1.3 min |
| 5 | **Role-play**, voice first. Before it, a one-turn spoken rehearsal: Jake says "Here, let me help you with that", the learner answers, then Mira's taarof note | **Role-play**, voice first; no taarof note before (the phrase tip taught it; Mira teaches it after if missed) | ~1–1.5 min |

Mr. Patel's voice message moves to a later chapter. Each step opens with a short recall of earlier
phrases (spoken), as the framework says.

**Fix priorities per step** (the one fix lands on what the chapter teaches): retell (he/she for
Jake and Sara, past -ed); Quick-fire (a/an with jobs, like + -ing, past simple for a past time); About
you (am + verb, a/an, from/in/at); role-play (question word order).
