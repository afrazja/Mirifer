# Speaking first: steps that make the learner talk

Owner's request (2026-10-28): for learners who want to improve their speaking, the steps should make
them talk more, and the steps that do little for speaking should go. Status: **proposal, before
review.**

## Who it is for

The Everyday goal and "Not sure yet: I just want to speak better" (the default story for both).
Exam and Interview are speaking-only by design already. Travel keeps a listening step for now
(catching announcements and directions is part of that goal); its new chapters follow this rule too.

## How much each step makes the learner talk

Estimated from each step's design (its limits in code); the real data is too thin to judge yet (17
recordings in 90 days, and role-play replies aren't measured at all).

| Step type | Learner's own voice per step | Kind of speaking | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listen and connect | **0 s** (taps answers) | none | Replace |
| Persian-speaker traps | ~15–20 s (says 5 fixed sentences) | repeating | Fold into other steps |
| Natural phrases | ~20–30 s (repeats 5 sentences) | repeating | Keep, add "use it" |
| Say it in English | ~30–40 s (3–4 short lines) | guided, own words | Replace by Quick-fire |
| Role-play with a twist | ~40–60 s, **can be typed** | conversation | Keep, voice first |
| Voice messages | ~45 s (2 replies) | own words | Keep in some chapters |
| Listen and retell | ~45–60 s | own words | Keep, also at A2 (shorter) |
| Say it again, better ("About you") | ~1.5–2 min (two tries) | own words + fix | Keep, both levels |
| Quick-fire questions (lab) | ~1.5 min (6 answers up to 20 s) | conversation | Add |
| Speaking card (lab) | ~1–2 min | long turn | Later chapters |
| 60-second free talk (lab) | ~1 min | long turn | Capstones |

Today a chapter gives about **2.5–3 minutes** of the learner's own speech (Intermediate) and most of
it is repeating. The proposal below gives about **6–7 minutes**, most of it in their own words.

## The rule

1. **Every step makes the learner talk for at least a minute,** in their own words for most of it.
2. **Listening leads to speaking:** never tap-only questions; you listen, then you tell.
3. **Corrections come from the learner's own mistakes** (try → fix → try again), not from a list of
   other people's mistakes. The cultural items (taarof, compliments) move into the role-play and
   Mira's notes.
4. **Voice first:** the role-play's main button is the microphone; typing is a quiet fallback.
5. **Measure it:** each step records the learner's talk time, so the admin sees which steps make
   people talk, and we tune with real data.

## The new chapter shape (Everyday, both levels)

| | Step | Talk time | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Listen and tell**: hear the scene (about 45 s), then say what happened in 2–3 sentences (A2: with three picture/keyword hints; B1: free retell) | ~45–60 s | Replaces Listen and connect (A2); B1 keeps its retell |
| 2 | **Natural phrases + use them**: repeat five phrases, then answer two personal questions using them | ~60 s | Adds a "use it" part |
| 3 | **About you** (Say it again, better): try → Mira's one or two fixes → try again | ~1.5 min | Now for A2 too (shorter, 45 s), replaces Traps |
| 4 | **Quick-fire with Jake**: six short questions from the neighbor, answer in one or two sentences each | ~1.5 min | Replaces Say it in English (A2) and Voice messages (B1 moves to a later chapter) |
| 5 | **Role-play with a twist**, voice first | ~1–1.5 min | Mic is the main button; typing hidden behind "Type instead" |

The taarof and compliment "which reply?" items stay in the chapter as **Mira's quick note before the
role-play** (one screen, one example, then Start), so the cultural point is still taught right before
it's used, without a separate tapping step.
