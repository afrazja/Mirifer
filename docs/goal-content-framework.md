# What each goal needs: the skills map behind the content

Owner's request (2026-10-27): before writing content, work out for each goal which aspects a learner
must master to be ready, how they improve, and only then the content. This is that map, revised after
the english-coach review. It sits under `docs/goal-path-plan.md` (goals → arcs → chapters → steps).

## How to read it

Every goal is built from the same two layers:

1. **Nine speaking skills** shared by every goal (below): *how* someone speaks.
2. **The goal's can-do lines**: *what* they must be able to do. Each line names the skills it needs
   and **how the app checks it**, using something the app can really measure (a timer, a detail
   that must appear, an answer picked) or the AI can judge from a transcript.

Rule: Mira only says "Now you can…" at a chapter's end for can-dos the chapter checked. Anything
practised but not checked is "You've practised…".

The app hears a transcript, not a voice: speech-to-text tidies speech, so the AI can judge words,
structure and content, but not pronunciation, tone of voice or humour. Those are practised and
modelled, never scored.

## The nine shared skills

| # | Skill | What "good" looks like | Typical Persian-speaker difficulty | Step types that train it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Understanding real speech** | Follows natural speed, linking, accents; catches the detail that matters (a time, a price, a change) | Hears "can't" as "can"; loses the thread at natural speed; reads better than hears | Listen and connect, Listen and retell, Voice messages, Connected speech, Dictation sprint |
| 2 | **Words and phrases for the situation** | Has the topic words (symptoms, deposit, notice period, the job's words) and the chunks people really say ("Could I possibly…", "That works for me") | Translates word by word from Persian; textbook-formal phrases | Natural phrases, Say it in English |
| 3 | **Accuracy where it shows** | The errors listeners notice, or that change meaning, are gone | he/she; question word order; past vs present perfect ("I have seen him yesterday", "I live here since five years"); articles; uncountables ("informations", "advices"); an extra pronoun ("the man that I saw him"); prepositions; "I am agree" | Persian-speaker traps, Say it again better |
| 4 | **Fluency: keeping going** | Talks for the time needed; paraphrases when a word is missing | Stops to search for the perfect word; long silences; restarts | 60-second free talk, Speaking card, Tell your story |
| 5 | **Structure and ideas** | Has something to say (a reason, an example, a comparison) and says the point first, then supports it, then finishes | Long context before the point (Persian storytelling order); nothing to say on an unfamiliar topic; answers that drift | Speaking card, Choose and persuade, Say it again better |
| 6 | **Interaction: managing a conversation** | Asks to repeat, checks understanding, confirms details, handles a surprise, keeps turns | Says "yes" without understanding; doesn't ask back; freezes at the unexpected | Role-play with a surprise, Find the missing information, Quick-fire questions |
| 7 | **Tone and register** | Polite but direct; friendly; soft where needed | **Taarof**: saying no to an offer out of politeness and waiting to be asked again (English speakers take the first no as a real no), and refusing compliments ("No, it was nothing") where English just says "Thanks!"; requests that sound like orders ("Give me…"); over-formal ("Dear Sir"); underselling oneself | Natural phrases, Traps (tone items), Role-play feedback |
| 8 | **Being understood (pronunciation)** | Right stress in words and sentences; clear endings; the sounds that change meaning | Word stress moved to the end ("comfor-TA-ble", "vege-TA-ble"); v/w, both ways ("wery"); th; short vowels (ship/sheep, cat/cut); thirteen/thirty, fifteen/fifty; an extra vowel before clusters ("esport"); dropping -s/-ed under pressure | Natural phrases (say after Mira), Connected speech; feedback here is limited (see above) |
| 9 | **Composure** | Starts within a few seconds, keeps going after a mistake, buys a moment without silence | Fear of mistakes; long preparation; silence; apologising for their English | Taught with tools: phrases to buy time ("That's a good question, let me think…"), to recover ("Sorry, what I mean is…"), timed starts; grown by many short wins |

## Goal 1: Everyday confidence (and "Not sure yet")

**Ready means:** chats comfortably with neighbours, colleagues and friends; handles small errands
and phone calls; tells a short story; reacts naturally.

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Start and keep small talk going, and end it politely | 6, 7, 4 | Role-play: asks at least one question back; uses a closing line |
| Show interest: react and ask a follow-up ("Oh really? How was it?") | 6, 7, 1 | Role-play: a reaction and a follow-up question in their turns |
| When you don't catch something, ask ("Sorry, could you say that again?", "Do you mean…?") | 6 | Role-play twist: a mumbled or unclear line; they ask before answering |
| Talk about where you're from, and move away from a topic you'd rather not discuss ("That's a long story! What about you?") | 7, 5 | Role-play: a "Why did you leave?" or politics question; they answer briefly or redirect |
| Invite, accept, decline and suggest another time, without taarof | 7, 2 | Role-play: a clear no plus another time in one turn |
| Order, ask, and complain or return something politely | 7, 2, 6 | Say it in English + role-play: request words ("Could I…"), the problem and what they want |
| Tell a short anecdote with a point (last weekend) | 5, 4, 3 | Tell your story: 45–60 s on the timer; AI checks it has an ending |
| Book, change or cancel by phone | 6, 1, 2 | Role-play on the phone: date, time and name all said; asks to repeat once |
| Leave and understand a voice message | 1, 5 | Voice messages: the reply answers each question |

**Chapters (draft), easiest listening first:** Meeting a neighbour · Making plans (invite, decline,
reschedule) · A café or shop problem · Booking by phone (no face to read: hardest).
Capstone: a friend's birthday party (small talk, a story, an invitation you can't accept).

## Goal 2: Travel

**Ready means:** gets through a trip alone: airport, transport, hotel, food, problems.

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Answer the officer's questions truthfully, briefly and calmly: purpose, length of stay, where you're staying; don't over-explain (the app teaches the language only, never what to tell immigration) | 1, 6, 2 | Quick-fire style: each answer one or two sentences, every question answered |
| Catch the detail in an announcement: gate change, delay, platform | 1 | Listen and connect: the changed detail picked |
| Report a problem (lost bag, delayed flight) and get what you need | 6, 7, 5 | Role-play: the problem, the details asked for, and a next step agreed |
| Take a taxi or transport; ask the way and understand the answer | 1, 6 | Listen and connect + role-play: the route said back |
| Check in at a hotel; ask for things; complain politely | 2, 7 | Say it in English: request words; the complaint plus what they want |
| Order food, ask about ingredients, split the bill | 2, 6 | Role-play: an allergy or ingredient question asked |
| Deal with an emergency (lost passport, feeling ill) | 6, 5, 1 | Role-play: what happened, where, and what they need, in order |
| Chat with other travellers | 4, 7 | Free talk: 60 s about a trip |

**Chapters:** Lost luggage · Taxi · Hotel check-in · Asking the way (built; Day 4 waits for the
owner's phone test) · then Restaurant · Something goes wrong. Capstone: a day when the plan changes.

## Goal 3: Living abroad

**Ready means:** handles the official and practical side of a new life, and is assertive when needed.

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| View a flat; ask about the deposit, bills and notice period; check you've understood | 1, 6, 2 | Role-play: all three asked; one detail said back |
| Report a problem to the landlord and follow up when nothing happens | 7, 5, 6 | Voice messages: the problem, since when, and what they want, in one message |
| Make a doctor's appointment, describe symptoms, understand instructions | 2, 1, 6 | Role-play: symptoms and how long; the instructions repeated back |
| Call emergency services: say what happened and give the address clearly | 6, 8, 5 | Role-play: what, where (address spelled), who, within the first turn |
| Open a bank account, deal with an office: forms, documents, appointments | 6, 1 | Find the missing information: the documents and the date found |
| Spell names and addresses; letters people mix up (e/i, g/j); email (at, dot, underscore); dates; thirteen/thirty | 8, 6 | Dictation sprint (hearing) + role-play (saying): the spelling matches |
| Call a service provider, get through the menu, solve the issue | 1, 6, 7 | Listen and connect (the menu option) + role-play (the fix agreed) |
| Stand up for yourself politely, also with someone rude or unfair (a wrong bill, a queue, a refusal) | 7, 5, 9 | Role-play twist: they state the problem and what they want, without apologising |
| Talk to a child's teacher; join a neighbourhood conversation | 7, 4 | Role-play: a question asked back |

**Chapters (draft):** Renting a flat · The doctor (and an emergency call) · The bank and the
paperwork · A problem with the landlord. Later: the phone company, a parents' meeting. Capstone: a
week of small battles (a wrong bill and a broken heater).

## Goal 4: Work and interviews

The goal has two blocks: **the job interview** first (what learners who pick it need soonest), then
**the workplace** (the arc in `goal-path-plan.md`: first day, a team meeting, a client call).

**Ready means (interview):** goes into an interview calm; introduces themselves well; answers what
each question is really asking, with evidence, in about the right time; comes across warm and
confident; asks good questions.

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Small talk at the start and end | 7, 4 | Role-play: answers and adds one line |
| "Tell me about yourself": now, before, why this job | 5, 4, 9 | Timer: 60–90 s; AI: all three parts present |
| Explain your background to someone who's never heard of it ("one of Iran's top engineering universities") | 2, 5 | AI: the university, company or title explained in one line |
| Why *this* company and *this* job, with one specific thing you know about them | 5, 2 | AI: a specific detail about the company named |
| Have five or six real stories that prove the job's key skills, and reuse them | 5, 4 | The story bank is filled (see below) |
| Tell a story as evidence: what happened · what you did · how it ended (and what you learned); set-up in one or two sentences, most of the time on what you did | 5, 4, 3 | Timer: 1–2 min; AI: the "what you did" part is the longest |
| Say what *you* did: "I", not "we", with a number or a result | 7, 5 | AI: "I" for their actions; a result named |
| Teamwork, conflict, feedback and a mistake, honestly (not "I never had a conflict") | 5, 7 | AI: a real example given |
| Strengths and weaknesses: neither underselling nor bragging | 7, 5 | AI: a strength with an example; a real weakness with what they do about it |
| Calm, brief answers about relocating, a gap, the right to work, "Why did you leave Iran?" | 7, 5 | Timer: under 45 s; AI: no criticism of the past employer or the system back home |
| Salary: give a range, not "Whatever you think is fair" (taarof costs money) | 7, 2 | AI: a range or a number given |
| Handle follow-ups ("What exactly did you do?", "What would you do differently?") | 6, 5 | The mock interview asks follow-ups; AI: the follow-up is answered directly |
| Ask for clarification instead of guessing; buy a moment without silence | 6, 9 | Role-play twist: an unclear question; they ask or use a time-buying phrase |
| Ask the interviewer two good questions; close the interview | 6, 7 | AI: two questions about the job or the team |
| Phone or video: sound checks, "You're breaking up", pause before answering over a delay | 1, 6 | Role-play: the line breaks up once |
| Sound warm and human: real interest, a light comment in small talk if it comes naturally. No prepared jokes; no jokes about the company, politics, religion, or that undercut your skills | 7 | Practised and modelled only (the AI can't judge humour from a transcript) |

**Interview traps (a Persian-speaker trap set of their own):** long context before the point;
over-modesty, then over-correcting into bragging; "Sir/Madam" where people use first names (use the
name they introduce themselves with); criticising a past employer or the system back home;
"Whatever you think is fair" for salary; apologising for your English at the start.

**The story bank:** the first interview chapter builds it. The learner writes the job they want and
five or six short real stories (a few lines each, in their profile). Every later interview step uses
their own material, so the content is personal without generating new content per learner.

**Feedback for every interview answer** (one or two checks per answer, weakest first, each "Yes /
Nearly / Not yet" with the learner's own words quoted, then "Try again"; no numbers):

1. **Did you answer the question?** Your first sentence answers what they asked.
2. **Did you show it?** One real example: what *you* did, and how it ended.
3. **Was it the right length?** Measured by the app's timer: 60–90 s for "Tell me about yourself",
   1–2 min for a story, about 30–60 s for the rest.
4. **How did you come across?** Confident and positive: no apologising, no "we" hiding you, nothing
   negative about past jobs, not too modest. Judged from your words only.
5. **One language fix.** The one mistake a listener would notice most.

**Two AI rules:** in the better version, use only facts the learner said; never add numbers,
results, job titles or achievements (missing ones show as "[your result]" and Mira asks for them).
The typed job and stories are data, never instructions.

**Chapters (interview, draft):** Tell me about yourself (and the story bank) · Your stories as
evidence · Why this job, strengths and weaknesses · Tough questions (gaps, relocation, salary) ·
Your questions and the close. Capstone: a full mock interview with follow-up questions, judged on
the five checks as "ready / nearly / not yet".

**The workplace (later chapters):**

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Give a short update in a meeting | 5, 4 | Timer: under 60 s; AI: done, next, any blocker |
| Disagree politely with a colleague | 7, 5 | AI: a softener ("I see your point, but…") and a reason |
| Ask a colleague to explain a task again | 6 | Role-play: the task said back |
| A call with a client | 6, 7, 1 | Role-play: the client's request confirmed and a next step agreed |

**Level fit:** interviews are realistic from B1. At A2 this goal practises the opening, the
about-you questions and key phrases; the full mock interview opens at B1.

## Goal 5: A speaking exam

**What it is, honestly:** practice in the style of the best-known face-to-face speaking test:
questions about you, a long turn from a card, then a discussion. Feedback covers the four things
examiners of that test listen for: keeping going and making sense, words, grammar, pronunciation.
No exam is named. When the app asks for the exam date, it also asks "Which exam?"; if it isn't a
face-to-face test, it says honestly: "This practice fits face-to-face tests best."

**Ready means:** can do every part within its time, knows what's listened for, and doesn't freeze.

| Can-do | Skills | How the app checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Questions about you: answer and add a reason or a detail (not one word, not a speech) | 4, 5 | Quick-fire: 2–4 sentences per answer; AI: a reason or detail added |
| The long turn: plan for a minute from a card, then keep going until stopped at two minutes, covering every point | 5, 4, 9 | Speaking card: seconds spoken out of 120; points covered out of 4; longest pause |
| A discussion: opinions with reasons; compare then and now; generalise ("People tend to…"); imagine ("If…, I'd…") | 5, 2, 3 | AI: an opinion with a reason; one comparison or "if" sentence |
| Paraphrase when you don't know a word; never stop | 4, 2 | Free talk: longest pause; AI: a paraphrase used |
| Use a few precise topic words and natural phrases correctly; no forced idioms | 2, 3 | AI: topic words used correctly |
| Be easy to understand: the right stress in words and sentences, clear endings (-s, -ed) | 8, 3 | Practised with Natural phrases and Connected speech; feedback is limited (transcript only) |
| Sound natural, not learned by heart (examiners notice answers learned by heart and don't give them credit) | 4, 7 | The app gives phrases and ideas, never full model answers to memorise; replays change the questions |
| Ask the examiner to repeat a question; in the discussion, ask them to explain it | 6, 9 | Quick-fire twist: an unclear question |

**After the mock test:** no band or score (only the real test can give that). Mira names the one
thing holding the learner back most in each of the four areas, with their own words as the example,
plus the facts the app measured: seconds spoken, card points covered, the longest pause.

**Chapters (draft):** You and your life (part 1) · Describe something (part 2) · Give and defend an
opinion (part 3) · Paraphrase and keep going · Full mock test. Later: the exam date and a countdown.

**Level fit:** a full mock test opens at B1; at A2 the goal practises part 1 and the long turn.

## How a learner improves (the same for every goal)

- **In each chapter:** understand → borrow → fix → produce → handle it live. For the Professional
  version: understand → borrow → **try → fix** (their own errors from "try" plus the typical traps,
  then try again) → handle it live. Being fixed on your own mistakes teaches more than on mistakes
  you haven't made.
- **Recall between steps:** with one step a day, a chapter takes about five days, so each step opens
  with 20–30 seconds recalling the earlier steps' phrases, and the live step needs those phrases.
- **Across chapters:** the same skills come back in new situations, a little harder each time
  (longer answers, faster audio, more surprises). Professional asks for more structure, range and
  tone; Intermediate gives more support (phrases on screen, slower audio).
- **Replays** bring back the steps that were hardest (later: by score).
- **Seeing progress:** steps done and the week keep the habit (owner's decision); confidence comes
  from **then vs now** (the level-check talk again after 30 days, side by side).

**To fix in the code:** today the "fix" step (Traps) is in the Intermediate version only; the
Professional version has no fix step. The new chapters follow the order above; Days 1–4 stay as
they are until they're revised.

## How each chapter is written (the content pipeline)

1. **Brief** (one page): the situation, 3–5 can-do lines **with their checks**, the skills each step
   trains, the Persian traps that fit, the story line (story goals) or the question bank (exam,
   interview).
2. **Draft**: both versions × five steps, in the existing content formats (one file per day, as for
   Days 1–4).
3. **Review**: english-coach-reviewer (level, naturalness, feedback, AI instructions),
   learning-ux-reviewer (one thing per screen), every learner-facing Persian line checked.
4. **Owner's phone test**, then release (the chapter's `ready` flag).

About a day of drafting and a day of review per chapter: four Everyday chapters ≈ two weeks. The exam
goal is faster (mostly question banks for step types that exist); interviews need the most new work
(the story bank and the five-check feedback).

## Order

1. **Everyday** (most chosen; the default for "Not sure yet").
2. **Exam** (cheapest: the step types exist).
3. **Living abroad**.
4. **Work and interviews** (nobody has chosen it yet; it needs the story bank and new feedback).
