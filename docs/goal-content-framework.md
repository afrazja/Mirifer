# What each goal needs: the skills map behind the content

Owner's request (2026-10-27): before writing content, work out for each goal which aspects a learner
must master to be ready, how they improve, and only then the content. This is that map. It sits under
`docs/goal-path-plan.md` (goals → arcs → chapters → steps).

## How to read it

Every goal is built from the same two layers:

1. **Nine speaking skills** shared by every goal (below). They are *how* someone speaks.
2. **The goal's situations and tasks**: *what* they must be able to do ("can-do" lines), each one
   practised by the skills that matter most for it.

A chapter = one situation. Its five steps each train one or two skills on that situation, always in
the same order: **understand → borrow language → avoid traps → say it yourself → handle it live**.
The last step of every arc is a **capstone**: the whole situation from start to end, with a surprise
(the mock interview, a mock exam, a day abroad).

## The nine shared skills

| # | Skill | What "good" looks like | Typical Persian-speaker difficulty | Step types that train it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Understanding real speech** | Follows natural speed, linking, accents, and catches the detail that matters (a time, a price, a change) | Hears "can't" as "can"; loses the thread at natural speed; reads better than hears | Listen and connect, Listen and retell, Voice messages, Connected speech, Dictation sprint |
| 2 | **Ready-made language** | Has the chunks people really say in that situation ("Could I possibly…", "That works for me") | Translates word by word from Persian; textbook-formal phrases | Natural phrases, Say it in English |
| 3 | **Accuracy where it shows** | The errors that change meaning or sound odd are gone | Articles, he/she, tenses (present for past), prepositions, "I am agree", word order in questions | Persian-speaker traps, Say it again better |
| 4 | **Fluency: keeping going** | Talks for the time needed without freezing; repairs and paraphrases when a word is missing | Stops to search for the perfect word; long silences; restarts | 60-second free talk, Speaking card, Tell your story |
| 5 | **Structure: getting to the point** | Answers the question first, then supports it; signposts; finishes cleanly | Long context before the point (Persian storytelling order); answers drift or never end | Speaking card, Choose and persuade, Say it again better |
| 6 | **Interaction: managing a conversation** | Asks to repeat, checks understanding, confirms details, handles a surprise, keeps turns | Says "yes" without understanding; doesn't ask back; freezes at the unexpected | Role-play with a surprise, Find the missing information, Quick-fire questions |
| 7 | **Tone and register** | Polite but direct; friendly; soft where needed; a little humour when it fits | Taarof (refusing to be polite, which English takes literally); requests that sound like orders ("Give me…"); over-formal ("Dear Sir"); underselling oneself | Natural phrases, Traps (tone items), Role-play feedback |
| 8 | **Being understood (pronunciation)** | Stress on the right syllable and word; linked speech; the sounds that change meaning | w/v, th, short vowels (ship/sheep), extra vowel before clusters ("esport"), flat sentence stress | Natural phrases (say after Mira), Connected speech, a future pronunciation check |
| 9 | **Confidence** | Starts speaking quickly, keeps a steady voice, recovers from a mistake | Fear of mistakes; long preparation; silence | Not taught directly: grown by many short successes, timed speaking, and seeing progress |

Confidence (9) comes out of the others: short wins every step, speaking time that grows, and the
learner seeing their own progress (the week, steps done, later "then vs now").

## Goal 1: Everyday confidence (and "Not sure yet")

**Ready means:** chats comfortably with neighbours, colleagues and friends; handles small errands
and phone calls; tells a short story; reacts naturally.

| Can-do | Skills that matter most |
|---|---|
| Start and keep small talk going (weather, weekend, compliments), and end it politely | 6, 7, 4 |
| Show interest: react, ask follow-ups ("Oh really? How was it?") | 6, 7, 1 |
| Invite, accept, decline and suggest another time, without taarof | 7, 2 |
| Make a phone call: book, change or cancel an appointment | 6, 1, 2 |
| Order, ask about, and complain or return something politely | 7, 2, 6 |
| Tell a short anecdote (what happened last weekend) that has a point | 5, 4, 3 |
| Give an opinion softly and disagree without offending | 7, 5 |
| Leave and understand a voice message | 1, 5 |

**Chapters (draft):** Meeting a neighbour · Booking by phone · A café or shop problem · Making plans
(invite, decline, reschedule). Capstone: a friend's birthday party (small talk, a story, an
invitation you can't accept).

## Goal 2: Travel

**Ready means:** gets through a trip alone: airport, transport, hotel, food, problems.

| Can-do | Skills |
|---|---|
| Check in, go through security and passport control, answer the officer's questions | 1, 6, 2 |
| Report a problem (lost bag, delayed flight) and get what you need | 6, 7, 5 |
| Take a taxi or public transport; ask the way and understand the answer | 1, 6 |
| Check in at a hotel; ask for things; complain about the room politely | 2, 7 |
| Order food, ask about ingredients, split the bill | 2, 6 |
| Deal with an emergency (lost passport, feeling ill) | 6, 5, 1 |
| Chat with other travellers | 4, 7 |

**Chapters:** Lost luggage · Taxi · Hotel check-in · Asking the way (built, Day 4 not released) ·
then Restaurant · Something goes wrong (pharmacy or lost passport). Capstone: a day when the plan
changes.

## Goal 3: Living abroad

**Ready means:** handles the official and practical side of a new life, and is assertive when needed.

| Can-do | Skills |
|---|---|
| View a flat, ask the right questions, understand a contract's main terms | 1, 6, 2 |
| Report a problem to the landlord and follow up when nothing happens | 7, 5, 6 |
| Make a doctor's appointment, describe symptoms, understand instructions | 2, 1, 6 |
| Open a bank account, deal with an office: forms, documents, appointments | 6, 1 |
| Spell names and addresses, give dates and numbers, confirm details | 8, 6 |
| Call a service provider (internet, phone), get through the menu and solve the issue | 1, 6, 7 |
| Talk to a child's teacher; join a neighbourhood conversation | 7, 4 |
| Stand up for yourself politely (a wrong bill, a queue, a refusal) | 7, 5 |

**Chapters (draft):** Renting a flat · The doctor · The bank and the paperwork · A problem with the
landlord. Later: the phone company, a parents' meeting. Capstone: a week of small battles (a wrong
bill and a broken heater).

## Goal 4: Work and interviews

**Ready means:** goes into an interview calm, introduces themselves well, answers what each question
is really asking, in about the right time, with a friendly, confident tone; asks good questions.

| Can-do | Skills |
|---|---|
| Small talk at the start and end of the interview | 7, 4 |
| "Tell me about yourself": a 60–90 second pitch (now, before, why this job) | 5, 4, 9 |
| Answer what the question is *for* (each question type has an aim: motivation, fit, evidence) | 5, 1 |
| Behavioural questions with a story: situation, task, action, result, in 1–2 minutes | 5, 4, 3 |
| Strengths and weaknesses without underselling or bragging | 7, 5 |
| Tricky questions: gaps, leaving a job, salary, "Why should we hire you?" | 5, 7 |
| Ask for clarification instead of guessing; buy a moment to think without silence | 6, 4 |
| A friendly tone and a little natural humour; no rehearsed-sounding script | 7, 9 |
| Ask the interviewer two good questions; close the interview | 6, 7 |
| Phone or video interview: start, sound checks, "You're breaking up" | 1, 6 |

**What is different:** no story. The content is the learner's own answers. They give their target job
(one line) at the first chapter. Mira asks; the feedback checks four things for every answer:
**answered the question asked · structure · length · tone**, then shows a better version in their
own words (the "Say it again better" pattern) and lets them try again.

**Chapters (draft):** Tell me about yourself · Your experience (stories) · Why this job, strengths
and weaknesses · Tough questions · Your questions and the close. Capstone: a full mock interview with
Mira as the interviewer, scored on the four checks.

## Goal 5: A speaking exam

**Ready means:** can do every part of a typical speaking test within its time, and knows how they
are judged. (No exam brand is named; the four public criteria used by the common tests are
fluency and coherence, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation.)

| Can-do | Skills |
|---|---|
| Short questions about yourself: answer and add one detail or reason (not one word, not a speech) | 4, 5 |
| A long turn: one minute to plan from a card, then up to two minutes, covering every point | 5, 4 |
| A discussion on abstract topics: opinions, reasons, comparisons, speculation ("It might be…") | 5, 2, 3 |
| Paraphrase when you don't know a word; never stop | 4, 2 |
| Show range: a few less common words and structures, used correctly | 2, 3 |
| Sound natural, not memorised (examiners mark memorised answers down) | 4, 7 |
| Manage time and nerves | 9, 4 |

**What is different:** no story, built mostly from step types that already exist in the lab:
Quick-fire questions (part 1), Speaking card (part 2), Choose and persuade / Say it again better
(part 3), 60-second free talk (fluency). The new work is question banks per topic and a feedback
that names the four criteria in plain words. Capstone: a full timed mock test with a rough band
estimate (clearly labelled as an estimate).

**Chapters (draft):** You and your life (part 1) · Describe something (part 2) · Give and defend an
opinion (part 3) · Paraphrase and keep going · Full mock test. Later: the exam date and a countdown.

## How a learner improves (the same for every goal)

- **In each chapter:** understand → borrow → fix → produce → handle it live (the five steps).
- **Across chapters:** the same skills come back in new situations, a little harder each time
  (longer answers, faster audio, more surprises). Professional versions ask for more structure, range
  and tone; Intermediate versions give more support (phrases on screen, slower audio).
- **Replays** bring back the steps that were hardest (later: by score).
- **Seeing progress:** steps done, the streak, and later a "then vs now" with the level check.

## How each chapter is written (the content pipeline)

1. **Brief** (one page): the situation, 3–5 can-do lines, the skills each step trains, the Persian
   traps that fit, the story line (for story goals) or the question bank (for "about you" goals).
2. **Draft**: both versions × five steps, in the existing content formats (one file per day, as for
   Days 1–4).
3. **Review**: english-coach-reviewer (level, naturalness, feedback), learning-ux-reviewer (one thing
   per screen), Persian checked for every learner-facing line.
4. **Owner's phone test**, then release (the chapter's `ready` flag).

One chapter is about a day of drafting and a day of review and fixes. Four Everyday chapters ≈ two
weeks. The exam goal is faster (mostly question banks for modules that exist).

## Order

1. **Everyday** (most chosen; the default for "Not sure yet").
2. **Exam** (cheapest: the step types exist).
3. **Living abroad**.
4. **Work and interviews** (nobody has chosen it yet; its feedback needs the most new work).
