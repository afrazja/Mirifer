# Day 4 world: "Asking the way"

The shared source for every Day 4 module. Every fact a module uses must come from here (or be
added here first), so the modules never contradict each other or Days 1–3. Theme 1.14 ("Asking the
way") in `docs/english-themes-plan.md`, week 1 "Your first trip". Goal of the day: **ask how to get
somewhere, and understand the answer** (A2). Understanding includes checking it: saying the way back
("So I turn left at the second light?") and asking for a repeat or slower speech. US English (US
voices; "block", "traffic light", "line"). Not a real city, place or company.

## Two tracks (owner's decision, as on Days 2–3)

- **Intermediate (A2):** short, guided speaking; recognising and repeating; short answers.
- **Professional (B1+):** own, longer speech; conversations; reasons and stories.

Same story for both; only the modules and how hard their content is change. The owner chose the
same five modules per version as Days 2–3 (see `README.md`), no end-of-day teaser, and one item
from an earlier day inside the lesson (see "From an earlier day" below).

## Where and when

**Riverton**, the same mid-size US city. **Sunday**, the day after Sara arrived (Days 2–3 were
Saturday). Sunny, warm, a light wind.

**The normal way (Daniel's):** turn right out of the hotel; walk straight along Main Street, past
the post office; at the **second traffic light**, at the bank, turn **left** onto Oak Street; walk
**two blocks**; the park's main gate is **on your right**, across from the City Museum; the fountain
and the market are just inside. **About 15 minutes.**

**Today's problem:** **Oak Street is closed** from Main Street to the park, **9 a.m. to 1 p.m.**, for
the **Riverton Fun Run** (a charity race). Police barriers at the corner of Main and Oak. People
can't walk on Oak Street while the runners pass. (Bus 4 also skips Oak Street until 1 p.m. and
stops at the Pine Street gate instead; only modules that mention the bus use this.)

**The way around (the stranger's):** don't turn at Oak Street; keep going straight on Main Street
to the **next traffic light**, **Pine Street**; turn **left**; go **past the library** (on your
left); after about five minutes, the park's **side gate is on your right**; inside, follow the
signs to the fountain (about 3 minutes). **About 20 minutes in all** from the hotel.

## The Sunday market

- In Central Park, **around the fountain**, every Sunday **9 a.m. to 2 p.m.**
- About 30 stalls: fruit and vegetables, bread, coffee, honey, flowers, local crafts, and food
  stalls for lunch. **Live music at noon**, by the fountain.
- Cash or card at most stalls.

## What happens (Sara's Sunday morning)

1. **7:30 a.m.** Breakfast at The Corner Café (included; 7 to 10:30 on weekends, Day 3).
2. **About 8:50.** The airline's van brings the green backpack. **Daniel calls room 714** (as he
   promised on Day 3). Sara picks it up at the desk, finds her charger, and **gives back the hotel's
   charger**.
3. **About 10:30.** Sara asks Daniel how to get to the Sunday market in Central Park. He gives the
   normal way (Oak Street) and says it's about 15 minutes on foot. She checks it back with him
   ("So right out of the hotel, then left at the second light?"). He forgets about the Fun Run.
4. **About 10:45.** At the corner of Main and Oak: police barriers, runners. She asks **Greg** at the
   bus stop. He speaks fast at first; she asks him to slow down; he gives the way around by Pine
   Street and the library. She says it back to him.
5. **About 11:00.** She reaches the side gate and follows the signs to the fountain. She's early for
   Tom (noon), so she walks around the market and buys a coffee.
6. **Noon.** Tom arrives by bus. Lunch is for Day 5 (not decided here; no Day 4 module says where).

## Topics for the learner's own speech (personal, never about Sara)

- Asking strangers for directions or using a map app: which do you do, and why?
- A time you got lost (in a new city, a building, or your own town), and how you found your way.
- Giving directions to someone: from your home to a place nearby (never a full address).
- Walking, the bus, or a taxi in a new city: what do you choose, and why?
- A market you like (in your city or one you visited).
