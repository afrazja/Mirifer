# English daily lessons: themes and sub-themes (v3.1, for the owner's choice)

Status: **v3.1, for the owner.** The owner's direction: a theme like "Travel" is too broad, so it is split into **sub-themes**, each one a concrete situation. **One sub-theme = one daily lesson.** The English-coach reviewer checked v3, and its points are merged (see the end).

## Two levels

- **Theme:** a family of situations, such as Travel or Health. A week of lessons stays in one theme and tells a small story ("your first trip").
- **Sub-theme:** one situation with one goal = one daily lesson. All of the day's modules are written for it: the listening conversation, the 5 phrases, your turn, and the scene with a twist in the 20-minute lesson.

**Levels:** A2 (easier), A2+, B1 (harder). ★ marks a good harder day (day 6 of a week).

**Goals have two actions,** and each action can be checked (listening questions, or what the learner says).

## Putting them in order (suggested)

A week stays in one theme and tells a small story. Days 1–5 are easier, day 6 is ★, and day 7 is a **big day**: a longer situation built from **the same week's situations** (never called "Review").

| Week | Theme | Days 1–6 | Day 7: big day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your first trip** | 1.6 Lost luggage *(built)* → 1.12 Taxi → 1.9 Hotel check-in → 1.14 Asking the way → 2.3 Ordering a meal → ★ 1.10 A problem with your room | The last day of the trip: a taxi, the hotel, finding your way and a meal |
| 2 | **Settling in** | 3.3 SIM card → 5.3 Bank account → 5.1 Finding a flat → 5.8 Neighbours → 2.7 Supermarket → ★ 5.2 Something's broken | Your first week in a new city |
| 3 | **Health and services** | 5.6 Spelling on the phone → 4.1 Booking by phone → 4.2 Doctor → 4.3 Pharmacy → 3.2 Returning something → ★ 3.5 A parcel didn't arrive | A day of errands |
| 4 | **Work** | 7.1 Your job → 7.8 Interview: about you → 7.2 First day → 7.3 Asking for help → 7.4 Video call → ★ 7.6 A work phone call | Your first week at work |
| 5 | **Social life** | 6.1 Meeting someone new → 6.2 Small talk → 6.3 Family → 6.5 Making plans → 6.6 Changing plans → ★ 6.8 A misunderstanding | A weekend with friends |
| 6 | **Exam-style speaking** | 8.1 → 8.2 → 8.3 → 8.4 → 8.5 → ★ 8.7 | An IELTS-style practice interview (no score) |

That's 42 days. The remaining 29 sub-themes make weeks 7–11.

**Note:** Day 1 (lost luggage, A2+) is a little harder than days 2–5 (A2). It stays as built, but expect lower completion on day 1.

**Week-1 trade-off (recommended: (a) now, (c) later):**
- **(a)** One story for everyone at launch. It's easier to judge with 30–50 learners. To keep work and exam learners interested, each week-1 "your turn" is personal, and work phrases show up naturally ("So, what do you do?" from the taxi driver).
- **(b)** A mixed week 1 loses the story.
- **(c)** Later, once day-7 data shows which onboarding reason drops off, week 2 can follow the learner's reason.

## Rules for every lesson

- **One goal, two actions,** and it must work in the 15-minute lesson (listening, phrases, your turn). The scene with a twist is 20-minute only.
- **Module slots per day:**
  - Listening (Listen and connect, Spot the lie or Dictation);
  - Phrases (5 sentences that tell a short story);
  - Your turn (Tell your story, Quick-fire, Speaking card or Choose and persuade, whichever fits);
  - 20 minutes: the scene with a twist.
- **Personal prompts don't assume a life:**
  - "your job, a job you'd like, or a family member's job";
  - "a time you moved, or your home now";
  - "a child in your family, or you at school";
  - health prompts about someone else or a trip, never stored.
- **Care with topics:**
  - home and city prompts stay on daily life, with no politics or news;
  - passport control and offices are neutral (at most "one more document"), with no visa advice;
  - forms ask only for name, address, date of birth and phone, with no residence or visa fields;
  - **bank:** the script never asks about nationality or refuses the account; it stays with ID, proof of address and a card;
  - **food:** "I don't eat pork", "I'm vegetarian" and "I have a nut allergy" are fine, but never with a religious reason;
  - **calling for help:** a minor injury only; show "In a real emergency, call your local number" and don't teach one country's number;
  - **a child's teacher:** the meeting stays positive, with no behaviour or discrimination stories;
  - **a misunderstanding:** a neutral cause (a short reply read the wrong way, a missed birthday), the friend isn't a partner, and nothing religious or political;
  - no assumptions about alcohol or dating;
  - "Where are you from? Iran" never gets a negative reaction or a stereotype, and learner characters have Iranian names;
  - avoid religion, hijab, salary and immigration status.
- **Nothing on screen promises a level.**
- **Build order:** finish week 1 (6 more days) before inviting the first 30–50 learners. Judge it by day-7 return and completion per day.

## What changed from v3 (English-coach review)

- **Week 1's big day** now reuses the week's situations; the airport trip home moves later.
- **Goals:**
  - two actions each (1.1, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8, 2.6, 6.7, 6.8, 7.6 rewritten);
  - every goal can be checked (4.4, 5.5, 6.1).
- **One situation per sub-theme:**
  - the gym only;
  - calling for help for a friend;
  - one Part 3 topic per day;
  - the flight attendant only.
- **Overlaps removed:** food delivery, the dentist and internet setup are dropped, and booking a flight became changing a booking.
- **Added:**
  - spelling on the phone;
  - leaving a voicemail;
  - registering with a doctor (replacing the vague "office forms");
  - talking to a university office.
- **Removed:** "the news of the day" from small talk.
- **New care rules:** the bank, food reasons, calling for help, a child's teacher, a misunderstanding, and forms.
- **Week order:**
  - week 2 in a realistic order (SIM, bank, flat);
  - week 4 has the interview before the first day;
  - week 5's ★ is the misunderstanding;
  - week 6's big day is called "IELTS-style", with no score.
- **Levels:** 6.1 is A2+, and the speaking card is about a minute.
