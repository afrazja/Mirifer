# English daily lessons: themes (draft for the owner's choice)

Status: **draft v1, for the owner to choose.** Each daily lesson has one theme and one goal ("By the end, you can…"). Modules are then written for the theme.

## How the 30 days are built

- **6 units of 5 days.** A unit is a theme family. Days 1–3 are easier, day 4 is a challenge (the same family, harder), and day 5 is a **review day**: the unit's phrases come back, plus one bigger scene.
- **24 theme days + 6 review days = 30 days.** This follows the course plan (`english-practice-architecture.md` §6).
- **The units match the onboarding reasons** (travel, everyday, living abroad, work, exam). Later, the learner's reason can decide which unit comes second.
- **Level:** A2 at the start, reaching B1 by unit 6. Each unit is a little harder than the one before.
- **Sounds** (Minimal pairs, Connected speech) don't depend on the theme: one sound focus per unit, which runs alongside.

## What each theme day needs (the module "slots")

Every theme day fills the same slots, so modules can be written per theme:

1. **Listening:** a short real-life conversation in the theme (Listen and connect, Spot the lie, or Dictation).
2. **Scene:** a role-play in the theme with a surprise (the scene with a twist).
3. **Phrases:** 5 natural sentences that tell a short theme story (Natural phrases).
4. **Your turn:** a personal story prompt in the theme (Tell your story, Speaking card, or Quick-fire).
5. **Optional for 20 minutes:** a reading (Read, rate, respond) or a sound game.

## Open choices for the owner

1. **Order:** one fixed path for everyone first (recommended: simpler to build and test), or start each learner with the unit that matches their reason?
2. **Unit 5 (exam-style):** keep it, or fold the exam topics into the other units?
3. **Any theme to add or drop?** For example, a visa or embassy interview, or school and children.
