# English daily lessons: themes and sub-themes (v3, for the owner's choice)

Status: **v3, for the owner.** The owner's direction: a theme like "Travel" is too broad, so it is split into **sub-themes**, each one a concrete situation (booking a hotel, at the airport…). **One sub-theme = one daily lesson.** v2's rules (one goal that fits 15 minutes, care with topics, week shape) still apply and are kept below.

## Two levels

- **Theme:** a family of situations, such as Travel or Health. A week of lessons usually stays in one theme, as a small story ("your first trip").
- **Sub-theme:** one situation with one goal, which is one daily lesson. All of the day's modules are written for it: the listening conversation, the 5 phrases, the scene with a twist, and your turn.

Levels: **A2** (easier), **A2+**, **B1** (harder). ★ = a good harder day (day 6 of a week).

**66 sub-themes in all.** That's more than two months of daily lessons. More can be added later (a visa or embassy interview handled with care, renting a car, a wedding…).

## Putting them in order (suggested)

A week stays in one theme and tells a small story. Days 1–5 are easier, day 6 is ★, and day 7 is a **big day** that joins the week's situations (never called "Review").

| Week | Theme | Days 1–6 | Day 7: big day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your first trip** | 1.6 Lost luggage *(built)* → 1.12 Taxis → 1.9 Hotel check-in → 1.14 Asking the way → 2.3 Ordering a meal → ★ 1.10 A problem with your room | The trip home: check-in, a gate change and a delay |
| 2 | **Settling in** | 5.1 Finding a flat → 5.3 Bank account → 3.3 SIM card → 5.8 Neighbours → 2.8 Supermarket → ★ 5.2 Something's broken | Your first week in a new city |
| 3 | **Health and services** | 4.1 Booking by phone → 4.2 Doctor → 4.3 Pharmacy → 3.1 Clothes → 3.2 Returning → ★ 3.5 A parcel didn't arrive | A day of errands |
| 4 | **Work** | 7.1 Your job → 7.2 First day → 7.3 Asking for help → 7.4 Video call → 7.7 Interview: about you → ★ 7.8 Interview: harder questions | Your first week at a new job |
| 5 | **Social life** | 6.1 Meeting someone new → 6.2 Small talk → 6.3 Family → 6.5 Making plans → 6.7 Celebration → ★ 6.6 Changing plans | A weekend with friends |
| 6 | **Exam-style speaking** | 8.1 → 8.2 → 8.3 → 8.4 → 8.5 → ★ 8.7 | A full practice interview |

That's 42 days. The rest of the catalogue makes up weeks 7–10.

**A trade-off to decide:** the product review warned that four travel days at the start suit "travel" learners but may lose "work" or "exam" learners in week 1, when most people drop. A week built as a story (one trip) holds together well, though. Options:
- **(a)** the order above, the same for everyone (simplest);
- **(b)** a week 1 that mixes themes;
- **(c)** later, week 2 picked by the learner's onboarding reason.

## Rules for every lesson (kept from v2)

- **One goal, two actions,** and it must work in the 15-minute lesson (listening, phrases, your turn). The scene with a twist is 20-minute only.
- **Module slots per day:**
  - Listening (Listen and connect, Spot the lie or Dictation);
  - Phrases (5 sentences that tell a short story);
  - Your turn (Tell your story, Quick-fire, Speaking card or Choose and persuade, whichever fits the day);
  - 20 minutes: the scene with a twist.
- **Personal prompts that don't assume a life:** "your job, a job you'd like, or a family member's job"; "a time you moved, or your home now"; health prompts about someone else or a trip, never stored.
- **Care with topics:**
  - home and city prompts stay on daily life, with no politics;
  - passport control and offices are neutral (at most "one more document"), with no visa advice;
  - no assumptions about alcohol or dating;
  - "Where are you from? Iran" never gets a negative reaction;
  - avoid religion, hijab, salary and immigration status.
- **Nothing on screen promises a level.**
- **Build order:** finish week 1 (6 more days) before inviting the first 30–50 learners. Judge it by day-7 return and completion per day.
