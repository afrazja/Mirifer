# Everyday Chapter 1 world: "Meeting a neighbor"

The shared source for every module of Everyday Chapter 1 (`README.md`, brief v2, approved). Every
fact a module uses must come from here (or be added here first). It must work as a learner's very
first chapter: few facts, nothing assumed from the travel chapters. US English ("neighbor",
"apartment", "backyard", "trash"). Not a real address or company.

## Two versions (as in every chapter)

- **Intermediate (A2):** short, guided speaking; recognising and repeating; short answers.
- **Professional (B1+):** own, longer speech; conversations; reasons and stories.

Same story for both; only the steps and how hard their content is change (`README.md`, "The five
steps").

## Where and when

**Riverton**, a mid-size US city (the same city as Sara's trip in the Travel chapters, never
required here). **48 Elm Street**, a small apartment building with stairs and no elevator. **A
Saturday morning**, about 10 a.m., in spring.

## People (and their voices)

Only four English voices: **Andrew** (a), **Ava** (b, always Mira, the coach), **Brian** (c),
**Emma** (d). Characters in the same scene never share a voice; Ava is only ever Mira.

| Who | Voice | About |
|---|---|---|
| **Sara** | Emma (d) | Came to Riverton on a trip, liked it, took a job here. Moved in this week; this morning she carries the last box upstairs. Friendly, a bit tired from moving. Her new apartment is on the fourth floor, next to Jake's (never needed by the learner). |
| **Jake** | Andrew (a) | Sara's and the learner's neighbor, **apartment 4B** (said once, never asked). A **nurse** (some weeks he works nights). Has lived in the building **six years**. Has a dog, **Biscuit**: friendly, just loud. Chatty, kind, makes small jokes, uses first names. |
| **Mr. Patel** | Brian (c) | The building manager. Only in the Professional voice message (step 4). Polite, a little formal, welcoming. |
| **The learner ("you")** | – | **Imagine** you've just moved into **apartment 3C**, one floor below Jake. You use **your own first name** (from onboarding, or any name you say); no module expects a particular name. Personal questions are about the learner's own life and must be true anywhere (where you're from, what you do, what you like doing). |

## What happens (Sara meets Jake, about 10 a.m.)

**Part 1, at the door:**

1. Sara is carrying a big box (mostly books) to the front door. Jake is coming back from a walk with
   Biscuit and holds the door.
2. Jake: "Here, let me help you with that." Sara: "Oh, no, it's fine!"
3. Jake takes her first no: "Oh, OK, no problem!" and starts up the stairs.
4. The box slips. Sara: "Actually… yes, please. That'd be great!" Jake: "Ha! Sure. Here."
5. They introduce themselves (first names). Biscuit barks: "That's Biscuit. He's friendly, just loud."
   Sara asks how long he's lived here: "Six years. You'll love it here."

**Part 2, upstairs:**

6. Jake tells her trash goes out Tuesday morning, by eight; the truck comes really early.
7. He sees her plant (it survived the move): "Nice plant!" Sara: "Thanks! I love it too." (No
   refusing the compliment.)
8. He invites her to the building barbecue in the backyard, a week from today at four. She'd love
   to come.
9. "See you around!"

## The learner's role-play (Imagine…, both versions)

The same meeting, with the learner instead of Sara: on the stairs, box in hand, Biscuit barking.
Jake offers to help; the learner would like help (the goal card says so). Jake says "Nice jacket!"
at some point. Intermediate: Jake says the trash line fast (audio only; the learner should ask him to
repeat). Professional: Jake asks a nosy question about the rent; the learner answers briefly or
steps around it. Details in `conversation.md`.

## Details some modules may use

- The learner is also carrying a heavy box, and a plant of their own (the compliment trap).
- In the phrases, the learner may ask Jake whether it's a nice neighborhood.

## Topics for the learner's own speech (personal, true anywhere)

- Where you're from, what you do, one thing you like doing (Professional "Say it again, better").
- What you'd ask a new neighbor.
- How people greet new neighbors where you're from.

## Facts that must stay true across modules

- 48 Elm Street; stairs, no elevator; a Saturday morning.
- Jake: 4B, a nurse, six years here, dog Biscuit. The learner: 3C, one floor below Jake.
- Trash: Tuesday morning, out by 8 a.m.
- The barbecue: the backyard, a week from today (Saturday) at 4 p.m.
