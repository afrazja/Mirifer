## The situation

**Where and when:** Riverton (the same mid-size US city as Sara's trip), **48 Elm Street**, a small
four-floor apartment building. A Saturday morning in spring. US English.

**The story line:** Sara came to Riverton on a trip and liked it so much that she took a job here.
She has just moved into **apartment 4A**. On her first morning, carrying the last boxes up, she
meets her neighbour from **4B**.

**Mira's opening line** (step 1 of the chapter; works whether or not the learner has met Sara):
"This is Sara. She came to Riverton on a trip, and now she lives here. She's just moved into a new
apartment, and today she meets her neighbour." · fa: «این ساراست. برای سفر به ریورتون آمد و حالا
همین‌جا زندگی می‌کند. تازه به یک آپارتمان جدید اسباب‌کشی کرده و امروز همسایه‌اش را می‌بیند.»

**People and voices** (four English voices; Ava is only ever Mira):

| Who | Voice | About |
|---|---|---|
| **Sara Demir** | Emma (d) | From Istanbul. New job at a design studio downtown. Friendly, a bit tired from moving. |
| **Jake**, apartment 4B | Andrew (a) | Has lived in the building for six years. A nurse (night shifts some weeks). Chatty, helpful, makes small jokes. Has a dog, **Biscuit**. |
| **Mr. Patel**, the building manager | Brian (c) | Only mentioned here (packages go to his office); he appears in later chapters. |
| **The learner ("you")** | – | Has also just moved in, to **apartment 3C**, one floor down. Gives their name as **Karimi** where a name is needed (as in the travel chapters). Personal questions are about the learner's own life. |

**Building facts** (every module uses only these):

| | |
|---|---|
| Recycling and trash | In the back yard. **Trash on Tuesday morning**, recycling every other Thursday. |
| Laundry | In the basement, **8 a.m. to 10 p.m.**; quarters or the laundry app. |
| Mail | Mailboxes in the entrance; packages go to the manager's office on the ground floor. |
| Quiet hours | After **10 p.m.** |
| The neighbourhood | A good coffee place round the corner: **Bean There**. The supermarket is two blocks away. |
| Building event | A **building barbecue in the back yard, next Saturday at 4 p.m.** (Jake mentions it). |

**What happens (the core conversation, about 90 seconds):** Jake holds the door and offers to help
with a box. Sara says "Oh, no, it's fine!" though she clearly needs help (taarof-like politeness),
and Jake takes it at face value. Then she laughs, changes her mind ("Actually, yes, please, that
would be great!"). They introduce themselves; Jake asks where she's from and why Riverton; she asks
how long he has lived here; he tells her about trash day and Bean There, invites her to the
barbecue, and compliments her plant. She says "Thanks!" (no refusing). She can't make the barbecue
time at first, and suggests coming a bit later. They part with "See you around!"

## The five steps

| | Intermediate (A2) | Professional (B1+) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Understand | **Listen and connect**: Sara meets Jake (the core conversation, A2 pace). Questions: Jake's apartment and job; trash day; what happened when Sara said "it's fine"; the barbecue day and time; what Sara says to the compliment | **Listen and retell**: Jake tells Sara about his first week in the building (a short funny story: the wrong apartment, a borrowed ladder), natural pace; retell it in 4–5 sentences |
| 2 Borrow | **Natural phrases** (A2, five): "Hi, I just moved in. I'm in 3C." · "Nice to meet you. I'm …" · "How long have you lived here?" · "Yes, please, that would be great!" · "Anyway, see you around!" | **Natural phrases** (B1, five): "I've just moved in down the hall." · "So what brought you to Riverton?" · "Oh, that's so kind. Actually, yes, I'd love some help." · "I'd love to, but I've got something at four. Could I come by a bit later?" · "Anyway, I'll let you get on. Nice talking to you!" |
| 3 Fix / Try → fix | **Persian-speaker traps** (five): "Where you are from?" → "Where are you from?" · "I live here since one week" → "I've lived here for a week" · he/she ("My neighbour is nurse. She works at night" said about Jake) · **taarof**: Jake offers help, you want it, which reply? · **a compliment**: "Nice plant!" → "Thanks!" not "No, it's nothing" | **Say it again, better** (try → fix): "Tell your new neighbour a little about yourself: where you're from, what you do, and why you're here." 45–60 s; Mira shows every mistake, practises the one or two that matter most, then a second try |
| 4 Say it yourself | **Say it in English** (four, Persian prompts): introduce yourself and your apartment · ask how long they've lived here · accept the offer to help · say you have to go, nicely | **Voice messages**: Jake leaves a message (the barbecue moved to Sunday, and could you bring a salad or drinks?). Reply: accept or decline politely, offer another time if you can't, answer his question |
| 5 Handle it live | **Role-play with a twist**: you meet Jake in the hallway, Biscuit barking. Goals: introduce yourself (name, 3C) · ask him something back · answer his offer to help clearly · close politely. **Twist:** Jake says the trash day fast ("Oh-and-trash-goes-out-Tuesdays-by-eight-'cause-they-come-early"): ask him to repeat | **Role-play with a twist**: the same meeting, B1. Goals: introduce yourself · ask about him · answer the offer · react to the barbecue invitation (accept, or decline with another time) · close. **Twist:** Jake asks "So why did you leave Iran?": answer briefly or redirect ("That's a long story! What about you, are you from here?") |

Order follows the framework: understand → borrow → fix → produce → handle it live; Professional
tries before it is fixed. Each step is about 3–4 minutes.

## Persian traps that fit (for Traps, the role-play's feedback and Mira's notes)

- **Taarof**: refusing an offer you want, waiting to be asked again. Jake takes the first no.
- **Refusing a compliment**: "No, it's nothing" where English says "Thanks!" (or "Thanks, it was a
  gift from my mum").
- **"Since" with time spans**, and the present for an ongoing time: "I live here since one week".
- **Question word order**: "Where you are from?", "How long you live here?".
- **he/she** for Jake.
- **Over-formal**: "Dear neighbour", "Mr. Jake". Neighbours use first names.

## Tone

Warm and light, a bit funny (Biscuit, Jake's jokes), never cartoonish. Jake's "Why did you leave
Iran?" is curious, not hostile, and the model answers show a short, honest reply and a friendly
redirect as equally fine. Nothing political in the model answers.
